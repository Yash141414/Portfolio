# Does a structural change explain the break?

Question: the frozen rules make 37.6% CAGR (Sharpe 2.31) from Sep 2025 to Oct 2026 but lose about 9% from Jan 2024
to Aug 2025. Did something about the contract change around September 2025 that the rules depend on? If so, the
strategy might only work under the current contract specs.

Short answer: **no structural change we can measure explains it.** Lot size can be ruled out. So can SEBI's November 2024
F&O changes. The move from Thursday to Tuesday expiry can't be ruled out completely, because it lines up exactly with
the start of the in-sample period. But the evidence points away from it. The profit doesn't start at the change: it
starts four months later, in January 2026, and three quarters of the DTE 1 profit comes from ten Mondays. The fair
description is that the rules fit one stretch, Jan–Oct 2026, not "the current contract specs".

All numbers below come from `report.md` in this folder (generated by `structural_break.py`) unless noted.

## What changed, and when

| Date | Change | Holdout (Jan 2023 – Aug 2025) | In-sample (Sep 2025 – Oct 2026) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Apr 2024 | Lot size 50 → 25 | both | no |
| 20 Nov 2024 | SEBI F&O rules: one weekly expiry per exchange, higher expiry-day margin, upfront premium; lot size 25 → 75 for new contracts | Nov 2024 – Aug 2025 | yes |
| 1 Sep 2025 | NIFTY weekly expiry Thursday → Tuesday (so DTE 1 moves from Wednesday to Monday) | no | **yes, the whole period** |
| Dec 2025 | Lot size 75 → 65 | no | Jan 2026 onwards |

The in-sample period starts on the same day as the Tuesday expiry. Every in-sample DTE 1 day is a Monday, and every
holdout DTE 1 day is a Wednesday (apart from 7 holiday weeks where it fell on a Tuesday). So a straight
in-sample/holdout comparison can't separate "the rules were overfitted" from "the rules only work with Tuesday
expiry". The checks below look for other ways to tell the two apart.

## 1. Lot size: ruled out

Sizing is a risk budget divided by worst-case loss per lot, so lot size only changes how finely positions can be
sized and when the 20-lot cap binds. Re-running both periods with every day's lot size replaced by 25, 50, 65 or 75:

| Combined CAGR / Sharpe | Actual | Lot 25 | Lot 50 | Lot 65 | Lot 75 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Holdout | +3.0% / 0.24 | +3.4% / 0.27 | +2.8% / 0.23 | +2.7% / 0.23 | +3.0% / 0.24 |
| In-sample | +37.6% / 2.31 | +37.0% / 2.22 | +35.6% / 2.18 | +35.6% / 2.25 | +36.6% / 2.33 |

The holdout with today's lot size (65) is still a Sharpe of 0.23, and the in-sample result barely moves at any lot size.
Lot size doesn't explain the break.

## 2. SEBI's November 2024 changes: ruled out

If the edge came from the post-SEBI market (one weekly expiry, bigger lots, higher expiry-day margins), the
holdout months after 20 Nov 2024 should look like in-sample. Apart from the expiry weekday, they have the same specs. They are
the worst part of the whole test:

| Combined | CAGR | Sharpe | Max DD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Holdout, Jan 2023 – 19 Nov 2024 (before SEBI changes) | +10.1% | 0.54 | -12.6% |
| Holdout, 20 Nov 2024 – Aug 2025 (after SEBI changes, lot 75, Thu expiry) | **-12.6%** | **-1.09** | -13.0% |

## 3. Tuesday expiry: not ruled out, but the evidence is against it

**The profit doesn't start when the expiry day changed.** Splitting the in-sample period at the 75 → 65 lot change:

| Combined, Tuesday expiry throughout | CAGR | Sharpe | Max DD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sep – Dec 2025 (lot 75) | +6.0% | 0.49 | -7.3% |
| Jan – Oct 2026 (lot 65) | +53.7% | 3.03 | -4.3% |

The first four months under Tuesday expiry look much like the holdout: a Sharpe of 0.49, against 0.54 for the holdout
before the SEBI changes. Nearly all of the in-sample result is 2026. Lot size can't be the reason for the jump
(check 1), so the edge shows up in January 2026, not at the spec change.

**The rule-free test shows no change in how options were priced.** A long ATM straddle bought at the DTE 1 signal
time (10:20) and sold at 15:15 tests whether near-expiry options were cheap or expensive relative to the moves that
followed. It doesn't depend on any rule:

| Long ATM straddle, DTE 1, 10:20 → 15:15 | Days | Cost (% of spot) | Avg return | Share of days > 0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Holdout, Wednesdays (Thu expiry) | 124 | 0.65–0.82% | +0.4% | 30–36% by year |
| In-sample, Mondays (Tue expiry) | 53 | 0.55–0.88% | +0.7% | 42–47% by year |

Monday DTE 1 options were priced about the same as Wednesday ones relative to the moves that followed. On expiry day
the straddle loses money in every period (-1% to -15% between 10:00 and 12:00). So selling premium on expiry
mornings paid in both regimes, yet Semi-Directional's expiry credit spread still lost in the holdout. That loss comes
from the rule's direction and timing, not from a change in option pricing.

**Monday has bigger gaps, but they don't explain the profit.** The one real difference with Tuesday expiry is that
DTE 1 now comes straight after the weekend. The average overnight gap on DTE 1 days is 0.48% in-sample (Mondays)
against 0.24% in the holdout (Wednesdays). The DTE 1 rules for Mean Reversion and Semi-Directional fade the move from
the previous close, so a bigger gap could mean more to fade. But the profit doesn't follow the gap:

| Avg combined DTE 1 P&L per day (₹) | Gap < 0.2% | 0.2–0.5% | > 0.5% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Holdout | -10 (59 days) | +16,241 (59) | +5,308 (13) |
| In-sample | +37,872 (18) | -1,722 (16) | +8,410 (18) |

In-sample, the DTE 1 profit comes from the **small-gap** Mondays, which are the ones most like a Wednesday. Ten of
those days in 2026 averaged ₹59.7k each, about ₹5.97 lakh, or **78% of the in-sample DTE 1 profit** of ₹7.64 lakh. If
the edge were "fade the weekend gap", it would show up on the large-gap days instead.

**Volatility doesn't separate the periods either.** Q1 2026, the best in-sample quarter (+₹7.08 lakh), had the
highest realised volatility in the data (17.6% annualised) and the largest overnight gaps (0.57%). But Q2 2024 (17.5%)
and Q2 2025 (16.1%) had similar volatility, and the book lost ₹1.82 lakh and ₹2.14 lakh in them.

## Conclusion

- The strategy doesn't only work under the current contract specs. Lot size makes no difference. The post-SEBI
  market, which is closest to today apart from the weekday, was the worst period. And the first four months of
  Tuesday expiry were no better than the holdout.
- What the in-sample result really reflects is January–October 2026, with most of the DTE 1 profit from ten Mondays.
  That is what you'd expect when rules are picked from thousands of candidates on one year.
- The expiry-day change remains an open question, because it is confounded with the fitted period and so can't be
  tested on history. Only forward data can test it. If the edge needs Tuesday expiry, it should show up in paper
  trading from 19 Oct 2026. The numbers above say not to expect it.

## Caveats

- The straddle test uses closes on 1-minute bars, with no costs or bid-ask spread. It compares periods with each
  other; it doesn't measure a tradable P&L.
- "Lot size replaced" changes only the contract multiplier used in sizing and P&L. It can't recreate how the market
  behaved under the older lot sizes.
- 2025-10-21 (Muhurat session, 105 bars) is skipped in-sample, as in the main backtest.
